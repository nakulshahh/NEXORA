## Why it exists

The feed is the textbook trade between work at write time and work at read time, driven by skewed follower counts.

<Callout kind="why">
One design question: where do you pay the cost of joining "posts" with "who follows whom"?
</Callout>

## How it works

### Numbers

Assume 10 million posts/day (~116 posts/s) and 200 followers on average.

- **Fan-out on write**: on each post, insert its id into each follower's feed list (a cache or table per user). 116 × 200 = **23 200 feed writes/s**. A read is one lookup, very fast.
- **Fan-out on read**: store posts per author. A read fetches the latest posts from each of ~200 followed accounts and merges them: at about 231 feed reads/s each merges ~200 timelines. Writes are trivial, reads are heavier.

### The celebrity problem

Follower counts are skewed. Writing one post to 50 million feeds is slow, bursty and wasteful (most followers are inactive). Hybrid: **push** for ordinary accounts, **pull** for very popular ones, and merge both when reading. Also skip pushing to inactive users, rebuilding their feed on return.

### Storage and paging

Keep per-user feeds as a bounded list of post ids (the newest few hundred) in a cache, with posts fetched by id. Page with a **cursor** (last seen id or time), not offset, so new posts do not shift pages (B3 pagination). Deletions and blocks are applied at read time by filtering.

### Ranking

Chronological is a sort. Ranked feeds score candidates with signals (recency, affinity, engagement), which adds a scoring stage and makes the result non-reproducible; keep it separate from retrieval.

## Common mistakes

<Callout kind="mistake">
Ignoring skew: a design verified with average follower counts collapses on the first account with millions.
</Callout>

- **Offset pagination on a changing list.**
- **Unbounded feed lists.**
- **Fan-out inside the request path** instead of a queue (F4).
- **Forgetting deletes and privacy changes.**

## Trade-offs

<Callout kind="tradeoff">
Push gives fast reads and heavy, bursty writes; pull gives light writes and heavier reads; hybrid has the best behaviour and the most moving parts.
</Callout>

## In production

<Callout kind="prod">
Monitor fan-out queue lag, feed read latency and cache hit rate, and set a follower threshold for switching strategy based on measurement.
</Callout>
