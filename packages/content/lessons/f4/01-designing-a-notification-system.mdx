## Why it exists

Many events (signup, order shipped, password reset) must tell a user something, by channels with different speeds and failure modes.

<Callout kind="why">
It combines queues (B7), events (D5), retries and preferences into one realistic system.
</Callout>

## How it works

1. **Trigger**: a service records an event and enqueues a notification job (ideally with the outbox pattern, so the event and the job commit together).
2. **Worker** loads the user's preferences, picks channel(s) and a template, and checks quiet hours and per-user rate limits.
3. **Send** to the provider (email service, push gateway) with a **deduplication key** such as `userId:eventId:channel`. Store the key and result so a retried job does not send twice.
4. **Retry** transient failures with exponential backoff and jitter (B7); after a limit, move to a **dead-letter queue** for inspection.
5. **Track** status (queued, sent, delivered, failed, bounced) and handle provider callbacks and unsubscribes.

Priority matters: password resets and security alerts use a fast queue; marketing digests use a slow, batched one, so a campaign cannot delay a login code.

Scale: a campaign to 5 million users is a fan-out job; chunk it, rate-limit to the provider's quota and spread over time.

## Common mistakes

<Callout kind="mistake">
Retrying a send after a timeout without a dedup key. The first attempt may have succeeded, and the user gets two emails.
</Callout>

- **Sending in the request path.**
- **One queue for urgent and bulk messages.**
- **Ignoring unsubscribe and bounce signals**, damaging sender reputation.
- **Putting secrets or full personal data in queue messages.**

## Trade-offs

<Callout kind="tradeoff">
At-least-once with dedup is simpler and robust; true exactly-once is rarely available end to end. Batching saves cost and delays delivery.
</Callout>

## In production

<Callout kind="prod">
Alert on queue age and dead-letter growth, monitor provider error rates, keep templates versioned and test with a sandbox provider.
</Callout>
