## Why it exists

A classic read-heavy design: write a long URL once, redirect to it many times.

<Callout kind="why">
It shows capacity arithmetic, id generation, caching and abuse in one small system.
</Callout>

## How it works

### Requirements and numbers

Create short link, redirect, optional expiry. Reads outnumber writes by perhaps 100:1; redirects must be fast and highly available. 100 million new links per month is ~40 writes/s; 100:1 reads is ~4 000 redirects/s.

### Code space

Base62 (0-9, a-z, A-Z) gives 62ⁿ codes, measured: length 6 → 5.68×10¹⁰, 7 → 3.52×10¹², 8 → 2.18×10¹⁴. Seven characters is plenty for decades at this rate.

### Generating codes

- **Counter then base62**: a unique id (database sequence or a range handed to each app instance) encoded in base62: 125 → "21", 10⁹ → "15FTGg". No collisions; codes are guessable and sequential, which may leak volume. Mitigate by scrambling the id with a reversible permutation.
- **Random**: pick 7 random characters; insert; on unique-constraint violation, retry. Measured chance the next insert collides: 2.8×10⁻⁷ at 10⁶ stored, 2.8×10⁻⁶ at 10⁷, 2.8×10⁻⁵ at 10⁸. Cheap, rare retries.
- **Hash of the URL**: deterministic (same URL, same code) but truncation collides (13 collisions among 200 000 at 5 characters, expected about 19), so you still need a check.

### Storage and redirect

One table: code (primary key), long URL, created, expiry, owner. Look up by primary key. Put a cache (B6) in front: hot links dominate. Use **302** if you want every click to reach you (analytics, edits), **301** when permanence and fewer requests matter; browsers cache 301 aggressively, so you cannot change the target later for those users.

### Abuse

Validate URLs and block dangerous schemes, rate-limit creation (C3), scan for phishing, prevent open redirects to internal addresses (SSRF when you preview links), and provide takedown.

## Common mistakes

<Callout kind="mistake">
Assuming uniqueness without a database constraint, then serving the wrong destination after a collision.
</Callout>

- **301 for links you may need to change.**
- **No cache** on a read-heavy path.
- **Sequential ids that expose business volume.**
- **Unbounded creation** by one client.

## Trade-offs

<Callout kind="tradeoff">
Counters avoid collisions and need coordination; random codes need no coordination and need retries; hashing deduplicates and still collides.
</Callout>

## In production

<Callout kind="prod">
Track cache hit ratio, redirect latency percentiles and creation rate; keep expiry cleanup as a batch job.
</Callout>
